    STAGE 1

    Data Preprocessing

    • Reads the Sunday exports as they come. Nobody on your team reformats anything first.
    • Finds do-not-contact, deceased and "no mail" wherever it's typed: the four do-not-market boxes, name fields, notes and the master DNC list. In the test run, rules alone caught all 53 "no mail" requests typed into notes. With your data we add an AI model for the phrasings rules miss, and a person confirms anything uncertain. Taking someone off a do-not-contact list always takes a person's sign-off.
    • Writes names and addresses one way, so "Kenneth/Jennifer," "HELEN AND CHRISTOPHER" and "1071 West Whisper Wood Way" all match their other records.
    • Links accounts into households by address, name and phone. It tolerates typos, never links across cities, and respects the Do Not Merge flag.
    STAGE 2

    Spatial Analysis

    • Puts every address on its county parcel. The parcel gives single-family or condo, the year built, and in most counties townhouse. In the test run it set the property type on 35,140 accounts. The 1,615 records not on a county roll are marked unverified for a person to check.
    • Keeps cities apart. TJ's example: the same house number and street in Dallas and Houston. They're two parcels, so suppressing one never suppresses the other.
    • Measures distance to ponds, canals and wetlands from the National Wetlands Inventory and county water layers, for mosquito targeting.
    • Finds neighbors: homes on streets where Massey already has customers but that are missing the service their neighbors buy.
    STAGE 3

    Machine Learning

    • Scores every household for its next best offer using the signals Massey listed in the Q&A: recent service, other services held, referrals, response to past mail, and years with only one service.
    • Starts with simple points on day one. The test run already ranks the termite list into 2,484 A, 3,799 B and 704 C households.
    • Learns from your response history once campaigns go out, and we report whether Tier A actually beats Tier C.
    • Predicts where mosquito demand jumps next from rain, forecasts and nearby water. That model is explained below.

    The mosquito model

    "It rained a lot in Houston."

    That was TJ's example of a perfect mosquito lead. It only works if the email goes out before the mosquitoes do. Floodwater mosquitoes emerge one to two weeks after heavy rain, sooner in hot weather, and TJ said a day's delay on his side "could actually be a week delay" in the campaign. The model's job is to spot the rain early enough for the email to land first.

    What it predicts

    For every zip code, every day in season: how many mosquito sign-ups and calls to expect over the next two weeks, and which households there to email first. Each morning you get a short list: which zips, which households, and why.

    What it learns from

    • Rain over the last 7 and 14 days against the 30-year normal (gridded National Weather Service rainfall)
    • The National Weather Service forecast for the next few days
    • Temperature, since warm weeks speed up the cycle
    • How close homes sit to ponds, canals and wetlands
    • Massey's own past mosquito sign-ups and leads, by zip and week
    • The household: services, home type, neighbors on mosquito service, past response

    How we build and check it

    A gradient-boosted tree model trained on past seasons of your sales and lead history, matched to the weather each week. It's the standard choice for data like this, and it reports which inputs drove each prediction. We test it on a season it never saw, and it only replaces the simple rule if it beats the rule.
